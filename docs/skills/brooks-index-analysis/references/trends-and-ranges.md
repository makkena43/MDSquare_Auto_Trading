# Trends and Trading Ranges: decision guidance

Version 0.6.0. Original operational synthesis from the supplied books. For reviewed page locators and limits, see [three-book coverage](three-book-coverage.md). Read the relevant sections when resolving phase, sequence, breakout-test, target or management questions. Keep one concise assessment, not a separate strategy engine per book.

## The books work together

Trends supplies bar interpretation, trend/channel structure, strength and evolving session types. Trading Ranges supplies breakout success/failure, magnets and measured moves, corrective sequences, ranges, order styles and trade mathematics/management. Reversals supplies the burden of evidence for changing direction, always-in interpretation and shared guidelines. None is the sole foundation.

Brooks describes discretionary choices beyond this project's baseline. User choices are completed-bar decisions, prospective stop-style entry plans, no new entries inside genuine tight ranges, price action without volume/OI/options, and bounded additions only under supplied policy. These are system adaptations, not claims that Brooks universally excludes volume, limit entries, early entries or all countertrend scalps.

## 1. Read pressure in its phase

Trends chapters 1–8, 12, 19; Ranges chapters 2–6.

An individual bar may be a breakout, spike or climax; context determines the dominant interpretation. Judge body/close quality, tails, relative size, overlap, progress and preceding extension together. A big bar initiating an accepted move differs from a big bar after a mature channel reaches opposition. Small bodies or opposite-color signals can qualify inside strong continuation context; attractive reversal bars do not override it.

For two-bar reversals with substantial overlap, test whether the proposed trigger clears the relevant combined structure rather than merely the second bar. Inside bars, ii/iii, ioi, outside bars and micro double tests are local descriptors. An outside bar is two-sided behavior and can demand a large stop; its breakout is not automatically a good entry. A micro double top in a strong bull spike can support a one-bar continuation flag, not a compulsory short. Micro double bottoms mirror this in bear spikes. A nested pattern does not require searching multiple timeframes to find a prettier chart.

Signal-bar appearance is not final before close under our policy. A prospective setup bar becomes a historical signal only if an entry actually occurs; the skill's prospective terminology must never imply a fill. Pattern evolution can create a genuinely new thesis; preserve old facts and episode/reentry rules rather than repeatedly renaming a losing signal.

## 2. Spike, tight channel, broad channel and range

Trends chapters 13–16, 19–23, 26; Ranges chapters 4, 11, 28–30.

| Developing behavior | Interpretive consequence | Current entry/holding consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong initiating spike | Displacement, small tails/overlap and follow-through indicate directional initiative | Consider registered breakout or first pullback; do not insist on H2 that never forms |
| Tight directional/microchannel | Small corrections retain strong directional pressure | First interruption often deserves continuation analysis, not automatic major reversal |
| Mature channel | More overlap, counter-bars and tests mean two-sided trading within remaining direction | Favor suitable corrective locations; a breakout at a new extreme needs fresh room/acceptance evidence |
| Broad channel/stairs | Trending extremes coexist with deep overlapping tests | Crossing back through the latest breakout boundary alone need not destroy the swing; defended structure matters |
| Independent range | Repeated rejection and two-sided acceptance dominate | Wide-edge rotation or abstention in tight/interior congestion |

A channel line break indicates increased two-sidedness; strength of the opposing move and subsequent test determine whether it is a correction, range or reversal. An overshoot of the opposite channel line can warn of exhaustion; sustained acceptance can instead indicate acceleration. Do not fade every line touch or apply a universal five-bar failure deadline.

Shrinking breakout progress in stairs is evidence of weakening momentum, not proof of the next reversal. Record the start of the channel as a possible later magnet. It is not necessarily an immediate target or a reason to short an intact bull channel.

## 3. Session profile without knowing the close

Trends chapters 21–26; Ranges chapter 15.

Keep the coarse regime separate from its evolving session narrative:

- Trend from open/small pullback: sustained initiative from observed opening structure, with corrections small relative to this session's preceding corrections. The eventual day extreme is not known yet.
- Spike and channel: initiative, first pause/test and less urgent continuing direction. Channel maturation can change fresh-entry quality without instantly invalidating an earlier holding.
- Trending ranges: breakout from one established range followed by another, with directional progress but meaningful two-sided tests. Returning into the earlier range is a specific change to evaluate, not automatically a new major trend.
- Trend resumption hypothesis: earlier initiative followed by extended consolidation; observe a qualified escape in either direction. Do not predict a late breakout or enter inside tight congestion because the morning trended.
- Reversal developing: credible new-direction pressure/acceptance after the earlier trend; the reversal-day label remains provisional until later evidence.
- Broad stairs: repeated progressing swings and overlapping breakout tests; watch declining extension and opposing strength.

Book examples of opening-range fractions, pullback ratios, frequency and US-clock inflection times remain source heuristics. No NSE threshold or timing edge follows without separate policy/testing. The main decision chart remains five-minute; completed higher-timeframe context clarifies a real ambiguity rather than becoming mandatory confluence.

## 4. Pullback hierarchy and actual attempts

Trends chapters 10, 16, 18, 20; Ranges chapters 11–14, 16–20, 26.

A first pullback can mean the first opposing bar, first minor-line test, first moving-average touch, first moving-average gap or first major-line break. Record which kind occurred. These are increasing signs of two-sidedness, not a mandatory ladder or immediate reversal. EMA-related observations require an available causal series; they are optional, and '20 gap bars' is a strength heuristic rather than a compulsory exact count.

H1/H2 and L1/L2 count actual attempted resumptions within an identified correction. A second higher-high/lower-low crossing requires intervening renewed correction and evidence of interruption, even if small. Consecutive bar crossings in an uninterrupted opposing channel can still belong to one corrective leg. Keep parent/nested attempts separate and expose ambiguous counts; do not pick the count that gives permission.

The same H2 has different meaning in a bull correction, at a wide range's lower edge, and inside a steep bear trend. A bar count is not a major reversal definition. Repeated failed attempts, including a failed H4/L4 interpretation, can challenge the parent premise; do not keep counting until one finally wins. Higher counts remain observations unless their setup definitions are enabled.

A double-bottom bull flag can be a two-legged pullback inside a bull trend; the visual double bottom does not make it a major bear-to-bull reversal. A wedge pullback points against the parent trend and resumes with it; a terminal wedge reversal points with the expiring trend and needs more opposing evidence. Three pushes suffice as an interpretation even without a perfect wedge outline, but pushes alone do not establish eligibility.

Dueling lines means a correction reaches independently known parent support/resistance; cite the levels before the turn, not after it. The 'two reasons' teaching means articulate contextual support plus setup/location evidence. Two correlated pattern labels cannot override context, and a powerful continuation first entry need not wait for an unrelated second indicator.

## 5. Breakout test versus return to the range

Trends chapters 3, 17, 22, 26; Ranges chapters 1–6, 20–23.

Assess breakout success against the proposed objective. A move enough for a small scalp may not support the requested measured swing. Price beyond a boundary can encounter fresh buying/selling or profit taking; the reaction, close quality and follow-through matter. A test can overshoot the broken boundary while continuation structure survives, especially in a broad channel. Repeated acceptance back inside, strong opposing displacement or loss of defended structure are different evidence.

Do not classify every pullback as a conclusively failed breakout. Brooks can describe the initial counter-move as a failure attempt; operationally keep attempt, confirmed return and failed failure separate. A weak reversal after a strong break can become a continuation test. A strong opposite move can instead justify transition. 'Trapped participants' remains inference, not observed order data.

Triangles and inside-bar clusters can indicate breakout mode, without specifying direction or success. Directional context may favor one side; genuine two-sided tight congestion still blocks inside entries. Observe a permitted confirmed escape/test. No automatic paired orders, doubled reverse orders or new triangle strategy follows.

## 6. Wide ranges require their own objective

Ranges chapters 17, 21–23; Trends chapters 1, 15, 17, 22.

Buy low/sell high describes location in an established wide range. Use supporting inward evidence and prospective confirmation with remaining room. A strong internal rally near the ceiling often deserves different treatment from a new bull initiative with outside acceptance. Take the intended internal objective under policy instead of hoping that every rotation becomes a breakout.

Range rotation can contain smaller trending legs and normal pullbacks; 'scalp' here does not always mean an exact few ticks or an exit at every opposing bar. A wide parent range can contain untradeable tight local congestion. A tiny flag within a strong trend is also a local range; the project can assess a registered escape outside it, while still prohibiting entries inside genuine tight congestion.

## 7. Measured-move geometries

Ranges chapters 6–10; Trends chapters 20–22, 25.

| Method | Causal geometry | Required premise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LEG_EQUALITY | Prior leg A→B, correction endpoint C; D=C+(B−A) | Confirmed leg and corrective endpoint, renewed directional thesis |
| SPIKE_EXTENSION | Spike start A and known endpoint B; D=B+(B−A) | Strong initiating spike; endpoint known through an observed pause or explicitly provisional while spike develops |
| RANGE_HEIGHT | H=upper−lower; upper+H upward or lower−H downward | Boundaries known before a supported breakout |
| MEASURING_GAP | Origin A, causally defined gap midpoint M; D=M+(M−A) | Plausible measuring gap within directional initiative, not a retrospectively labelled exhaustion gap |

The table groups range-height and measuring-gap projections under gap/range geometry. Signed formulas mirror down moves. For an intraday upward measuring gap, one possible gap is between broken resistance K and the first qualified pause/test low P; M=(K+P)/2. If P<K, this is a negative gap: record the ambiguity and weaker premise rather than treating it as clean acceptance. No market-profile or volume feed is required for these price-only calculations.

Spike open-to-close, high/low and corrective-endpoint variants can give different projections. Predeclare the convention and freeze the selected initial objective. Keep other causal projections as scenarios; never choose the one that matched the eventual turn. A strongly supported SPIKE_EXTENSION target is not identical to projecting leg equality from the later correction low. The latter subtracts the correction and produces a different objective.

Gap labels are provisional: breakaway, measuring or exhaustion cannot be known just because the gap exists. Known gap geometry can inform an assumption; later follow-through confirms or defeats it. Partial retracement changes the gap scenario and must be recorded at its actual cutoff. Existing filled targets do not move silently.

Targets are areas of possible reaction. Use the supplied tick/buffer policy for any exact executable level. A measured-move touch alone is neither a guaranteed reversal nor automatic permission for an opposite entry. Prior failed-reversal signal/entry levels and channel origins can supply opposing magnets if known at the cutoff. Compare the nearest meaningful alternatives; do not select the farthest objective merely to improve reward/risk. See [economics](trade-economics.md).

## 8. Trade mathematics and management fidelity

Ranges chapters 24–32; Trends chapters 8, 11, 18–19, 22–23.

Directional probability concerns an equidistant move. Objective-before-stop probability concerns this specific target, stop, entry and management policy. They differ. Brooks's discretionary 40/50/60% assumptions are teachings; the project keeps estimated probability null without compatible calibration. A good-looking setup, stronger always-in direction or the 80% inertia maxim cannot fill that field. Compute break-even probability and conditional scenarios instead.

Stop styles in the book include tactical signal/entry-bar, structural and monetary-distance stops. Our baseline is structural unless the supplied policy explicitly registers a tactical variant. A tight stop and a distant target require their own probability evidence; structural trend continuation does not guarantee survival of a tactical stop. Re-read eligibility after confirmation/latency changes the entry price.

For a swing long, a confirmed new higher low after renewed progress may support protective tightening under policy. A minor pullback low in a developing upper trading range is less defensible: routine range rotation can cross it. Premature breakeven protection can eject a healthy small-pullback trend trade. No automatic breakeven or trail rule follows from an example.

Reassess the remaining equation using current price, current stop, remaining objective and thesis evidence. The 'if flat, would I enter?' question is about economic desirability of the current exposure under comparable terms. It must not mechanically close a position simply because a new signal or category permission is absent. Persistent adverse acceptance or an unrealistic original objective needs an evidenced policy-authorized exit/reduction, not hope or a rescue addition.

Scaling into a corrective move differs from scaling into a failed countertrend idea. Planned adverse and favorable additions both increase exposure and require episode-level risk accounting. Adding to a potential major reversal is especially demanding and remains disabled without its separate policy. A better average does not erase total risk. No stop widening, automatic reversal, unlimited re-entry or new order style is enabled by source study. See [management](trade-management.md).

## Working output

Return context/swing/phase, relevant pressure, location, actual sequence, strongest contradiction, decision, and a permitted stop/target plan or next discriminator. For open trades, review supplied original facts and propose one management action. Keep all-book source detail in these references; the live decision remains compact.

## Wide-range rotation versus tight congestion: focused synthesis

Read only when the range interpretation changes eligibility or target choice. The range must be established with available outer boundaries and two-sided behavior; a weak-looking pause inside a strong trend is not automatically an independent range. Judge width relative to the relevant bars/corrective risk and usable inward travel after confirmation/costs, not a universal NSE point distance. Boundary uncertainty can make location unresolved even when bars look sideways.

In a persistent wide range, assess a qualified inward long near the lower edge or short near the upper edge. A strong approach to the edge can be a vacuum or can become an accepted breakout; its apparent momentum alone decides neither. Evaluate actual rejection/acceptance at the outer structure. A clear directional move within the range is not necessarily a breakout from the range. Do not chase it toward the opposite boundary because a local always-in bias looks strong. A prior trend bias can survive into balance while new swing entries become unsuitable.

Brooks describes range-edge H2/L2 and other inward patterns as well as failed-breakout entries (Trading Ranges PDF 715–718). Consequently, an explicitly registered range-rotation variant of `bull_high2` / `bear_low2` may qualify at the appropriate wide-range edge without an outside excursion. Require the real attempt sequence, inward confirmation, edge location, structural protection and configured inward objective. This is a new documented interpretation within existing IDs, not automatic activation or retrospective expansion of old trend-continuation-only policies. A `range_failed_breakout` route still requires an actual excursion and failure. Never invent one to fit the catalog. Category-off still overrides child-on; switching between variants cannot resurrect a consumed episode or evade a disabled route.

Range rotation aims at a supported internal objective under policy. Do not assume every entry will cross to the far edge or convert it into a breakout swing after entry. The midpoint can be a relevant fair-value/checkpoint area; it is not mandatory for every target. Other internal obstacles and the remaining distance after a delayed signal matter. An edge can be valid for observation yet no longer offer viable reward once the trigger is confirmable. Preserve that distinction rather than entering in the middle or tightening protection artificially.

Tight congestion remains no-new-entry under user scope. Book descriptions of limit entries, averaging and micro-scalps inside it are deliberately excluded. Keep observing a credible escape/test in either direction; a tiny boundary penetration is not enough. An open trend trade supplied from before the congestion is reviewed under its original premise/exit policy; the no-new-entry rule is not an automatic market close. Conversely, holding it does not authorize widening its stop while the balance expands.

A steep directional tight channel with continuing displacement and brief corrections can behave like a higher-timeframe spike. A flat overlapping channel with frequent reversals behaves like congestion. Source cautions against stop entries inside tight channels do not authorize buying any prior-bar crossing in the strong directional case: the project still requires its qualified pullback/escape route. Do not settle this ambiguity by the word 'tight' alone.

Repeated range-edge rejection supports continued two-sided interpretation, not a calibrated 80% forecast. Accepted outer escape can change context and invalidate a pending fade; no automatic reversal follows. Causal range bounds, the original inward objective and actual trade events remain distinct from a later expanded range. Losing a rotation does not prove a trend; succeeding does not prove the range must persist.
