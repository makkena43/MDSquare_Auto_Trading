# Brooks principles: operating doctrine

Version 0.6.0. Read this before classifying setups or managing supplied trades. This is an original synthesis, not Brooks impersonation or a proven mechanical strategy. The accompanying coverage register accounts for all 78 guidelines in Reversals chapter 25; that is not exhaustive review of every principle and chart in all three books. Source maps retain that distinction.

## Three-book foundation

Use Trends for bars, pressure, channels, continuation and evolving day profiles; Trading Ranges for breakouts/tests, corrections, magnets, measured moves, entries and management; Reversals for evidence of change and shared guidelines. [Cross-book decision guidance](trends-and-ranges.md) supplies distinctions from the first two books; [three-book coverage](three-book-coverage.md) records exact reviewed pages and remaining gaps. The 78-guideline register is one source layer, not the whole curriculum.

## 1. Market cycle and inertia

Read behavior first: breakout/impulse, directional channel, broad channel, established range or transition. Track daily background, intraday regime, persistent swing and local sequence separately. They need not point in the same direction. Trend flags are local ranges; determine whether they remain corrections inside a supported swing or have become independent two-sided congestion.

Continuation is the working hypothesis until price challenges it credibly. In a range, continuation means more rotation and failed escapes, not persistent direction. The often-cited 80% teaching is not an empirical probability for this instrument, stop or target. A successful breakout can change the working hypothesis. Do not use inertia to deny observable structural failure.

Day descriptions are provisional at the cutoff: trend from open, small-pullback trend, spike/channel, trending ranges, developing reversal or range-bound. Do not import the completed day's classification. See [regime](regime.md) and [swing context](swing-context.md).

## 2. Pressure and always-in interpretation

Infer buying/selling pressure from bodies, closes, tails, overlap, progress relative to earlier legs, pullback depth, repeated tests and follow-through. Several strong closes with sustained progress mean more than one attractive bar. Failure to advance when continuation was expected is useful opposing evidence. Evaluate what a move above/below the prior bar would reveal, without treating every bar as an entry.

Always-in bias means the direction favored if forced to choose: LONG, SHORT or UNCLEAR. It does not mean the account must hold a position. Report supporting and opposing observations with causal bar references. Do not substitute a numerical confidence score for calibrated success probability. EMA, if supplied and causally computed, is optional price-derived context; volume, OI and options are not required by this system.

## 3. Location, tests and acceptance

Map known swing highs/lows, prior session levels, boundaries, trend/channel lines and supplied round-number magnets. A level is a potential reaction location, not a guaranteed turning point. Distinguish touching, rejection, crossing and sustained acceptance. Projection anchors and lines must exist at the cutoff, not be redrawn using later turns.

Tight ranges prohibit new entries inside them. In wide established ranges, prefer confirmed inward opportunities near boundaries; skip the middle. An apparent range-edge bargain becomes a different hypothesis if strong outside acceptance develops. See [setup qualification](setups.md).

## 4. Context before pattern appearance

H1/H2 and L1/L2, double tests, wedges and nested patterns describe attempts; they do not carry fixed probabilities. First pullbacks in strong trends can be meaningful; second attempts are not universally better. Strong trend bars favor continuation where location and room support it. Dojis and weak closes need stronger contextual justification. An imperfect-looking signal may be valid; a beautiful countertrend bar may be a trap.

Interpret gaps, body gaps and microgaps as pressure observations where supplied prices support them, not new standalone strategies. Recognize relevant families, including disabled ones as observations, but retain one primary thesis per episode. Reading all principles does not enable unregistered setup IDs or bypass category switches.

## 5. Breakouts, failures and failed failures

For an excursion, distinguish continuation with acceptance, a profitable initial move followed by a normal test, and failure back into the old structure. A failed reversal can become a continuation entry; a failed breakout can become range rotation; a failure that itself fails can restore the original direction. Record which evidence would discriminate these alternatives.

Trapped traders and stop runs are explanations inferred from price behavior, not observed order inventories. No invented participant positioning, guaranteed second leg or unsupported probability follows. Climactic acceleration and overshoot can warn of exhaustion but are insufficient reasons to fade a strong move.

## 6. Minor and major reversals

Separate a pullback/opposing leg from a credible change of trend. Consider a meaningful structural challenge, strong opposing movement, test quality and acceptance in the new direction. A break of a line alone does not prove a major reversal. Nor must every initiating breakout wait for an identical multi-stage pattern: use its registered qualification rules.

Countertrend risk has a higher burden in a trend. Range-edge rotation is appropriate to range context. A small opposing signal can weaken an existing thesis without qualifying a new opposite trade. Exiting and entering the other direction are separate decisions; never automatically reverse a position.

## 7. Entry quality and trade equation

Separate signal identification, confirmation, prospective trigger, crossing and actual fill. Prefer sufficient confirmation without chasing a spent move. Refresh stale context before judging a sudden breakout. Stop, limit and market entries have different tradeoffs; current actionable contract uses prospective stop-style crossings. Other styles remain research until separately specified.

Evaluate probability, reward and risk together. For binary outcomes, expectancy = p × reward − (1−p) × risk − costs; the compatible costs and outcome model matter. High probability and a large reward relative to risk cannot both be assumed. Unknown p stays unknown. Do not borrow example probabilities or US instrument dollar/point examples. See [economics](trade-economics.md).

## 8. Expected move and target selection

Use causal swing-leg projection, range-height projection, opposition or range rotation as appropriate. Two legs and measured moves are hypotheses, not debts the market must pay. Targets require valid anchors, remaining room and a maintained structural premise. Distinguish a checkpoint from major opposition before entry. Keep the committed objective stable unless the frozen management policy permits an evidenced change.

A swing may pass a nearby checkpoint; a range rotation need not reach the far boundary. Avoid constructing a distant projection solely to improve reward/risk. Score target choice separately from signal identification and execution timing. See [swing context](swing-context.md).

## 9. Protection and open-trade management

Use structural protection compatible with the entry thesis and supplied risk policy. If a valid stop is farther away, sizing must fit the supplied budget; do not move the stop artificially nearer or invent size. Independent code must monitor actual protection and exits continuously. AI recommendations cannot guarantee timely stops.

Review current thesis, defended structure, target premise and opposing evidence after each completed decision bar. A normal pullback need not force an exit; confirmed invalidation outranks hope. Ask whether the remaining holding thesis is justified, rather than forcing an existing position to pass every fresh-entry gate. A valid holding can coexist with poor new-entry geometry. See [management](trade-management.md).

## 10. Scaling and consistent holding style

Scaling changes the episode's payoff distribution and risk, so it cannot be assessed by the initial entry equation alone. Adverse additions stay WATCH_ONLY unless count, sizes, total reserved risk, confirmation, expiry and unchanged common stop were declared before entry. Never add after invalidation, widen stops or use unlimited averaging.

Keep scalp/swing target and holding modes explicit. Do not turn a losing scalp into an indefinite swing or abandon a measured-swing plan reflexively at its first checkpoint. Reductions, break-even moves and trailing rules are policy-dependent; book examples do not silently become mandatory rules.

## 11. Discipline and execution realism

Wait when evidence is unclear. Boredom, recent profit/loss, a missed trade or a desired monthly count cannot create eligibility. Apply the same frozen criteria across the evaluation series; selection means context-based eligibility, not hindsight cherry-picking. Record bad fills, latency, ambiguity and missed triggers separately from interpretive quality.

Use one primary five-minute decision chart; completed larger-timeframe context can clarify structure without requiring multiple-chart agreement. NSE session timing and supplied operational risk controls replace US-clock examples. Known scheduled risk events may be handled by explicit policy; no directional news prediction or new news feed is required. Personal confidence, aspirations and position-growth advice do not authorize leverage or risk changes.

## 12. Learning and uncertainty

Chart interpretation and disciplined execution are separate capabilities. Patterns are approximate and markets fractal, but empirical edge does not transfer automatically between instruments. Maintain the strongest alternative and next observable discriminator. After an error, revise the explanation with evidence rather than rewriting the earlier decision.

Practice with later candles hidden. Preserve inspected dates as development data, freeze policy before a new evaluation and retain losses/no-trade periods. Neither chart annotation nor chapter completeness proves profitability. The coverage register is for development audits, not a 78-item checklist repeated at every candle.

## Scope and remaining source work

User scope is underlying price action without volume/OI/options filters. Volume-specific chapters, options, tick scalping, US position-size examples and automatic account growth are deliberately excluded. Additional chapter/chart study should add sourced interpretation and causal cases, not silently enable trades. Numerical operational rules, calibration and forward evidence remain separate work. This doctrine supplies guidance; application state, validators and execution are still specifications.
