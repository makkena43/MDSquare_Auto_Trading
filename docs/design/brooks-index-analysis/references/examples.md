# Contrasting context examples

Original synthetic qualitative examples, not market observations, expected returns or independently scored tests. They illustrate interpretation; they do not provide enough numeric/policy detail to issue JSON CANDIDATE. In structured use, eligible developing ideas remain WAIT until complete. Use source charts/data only when actually supplied; do not invent bars from these descriptions.

## Same H2 sequence, different context

- Bull trend; orderly correction holds a previously identified test area; completed second-attempt signal; first resistance still offers room. Interpretation: supported continuation hypothesis, eligible pilot family. Confirm sequence/current price/policy before a complete plan.
- Strong bear trend; small upward H2; no meaningful opposing structural change. Interpretation: countertrend attempt, not bull continuation. SKIP that long thesis; an H2 count is not evidence the bear trend ended.
- Wide range; H2 near lower edge after an actual failed downside excursion and return inside. Interpretation: range rotation; range_failed_breakout_long is primary when enabled. H2 supports the episode, not a second trade.
- Same count near wide-range upper edge or middle. Interpretation: poor long location unless a distinct accepted-breakout thesis becomes supported. SKIP the range long.
- Bull background but local two-sided tight congestion. Interpretation: no candidate inside the tight range. WAIT for credible escape; do not override with daily direction or the count.

## Same strong candle, different context

- Strong candle exits a previously identified range, follow-through holds outside and an actual controlled test forms. Interpretation: changing context may support breakout-pullback when its resumption signal is complete.
- Strong candle reaches an established wide-range upper edge, then fails to hold outside and returns inside. Interpretation: range persistence may support the enabled short range-failure thesis; do not chase the original bull candle.
- Strong candle closes just outside a tight range but no acceptance/test is yet available. Interpretation: observe attempted escape, WAIT; no automatic breakout-pullback candidate.
- Strong candle late in a directional extension near opposition, no meaningful pullback. Interpretation: initiating breakout versus exhaustion remains unresolved. Do not infer either from bar size alone; WAIT/SKIP a chased continuation.

## Same wedge, different thesis

- Three corrective pushes against an intact bull context near support. Interpretation: potential bull wedge pullback, observation-only until that family is enabled; a valid H2 may independently qualify only if its actual sequence/context exists.
- Three upward pushes after an extended bull move at opposition with meaningful bearish evidence. Interpretation: potential wedge reversal, observation-only under defaults. It is not the corrective wedge above.
- Three small pushes in two-sided tight overlap. Interpretation: forbidden congestion; no new candidate regardless of wedge label.

## Minor break versus contextual change

- One opposing bar breaks a short trend line while the main trend structure remains intact. Interpretation: correction first; do not declare major reversal.
- Opposing displacement materially challenges prior structure, follow-through persists and a subsequent test supports the new direction. Interpretation: reassess transition. Do not insist on the old direction because of the 80% heuristic; evaluate only eligible fully defined theses.

## Temporal and switch discipline

- A favorable signal's trigger crossed before the new plan could be accepted. Interpretation: missed/already-triggered, no retroactive entry. A genuinely new setup requires supplied re-entry rules.
- Same episode is called H2 on one candle and breakout-pullback on the next. Interpretation: one opportunity, changed evidence tags; no duplicate exposure or silently renewed expiry.
- Attractive reversal category is off. Interpretation: observation only. An enabled label cannot disguise that disabled thesis.

## Outcome is not the decision label

A well-supported continuation can fail; a sensible countertrend rejection can miss a winner. Evaluate whether evidence and policy supported the decision at its cutoff before looking at outcome. Include both kinds in numerical research cases. These examples do not establish 80%, any other probability or guaranteed success.

## Open-trade management contrasts

- Filled continuation long, normal corrective bars, relevant support/stop intact, target not reached: HOLD rather than close merely because one bar is bearish. New continuation entries still need their own eligibility.
- Same trade, meaningful opposing acceptance defeats the defended breakout premise: CLOSE or policy-defined REDUCE based on supplied state; do not average into the failed thesis.
- Filled wide-range rotation reaches its committed internal objective: TAKE_PROFIT under fixed-target policy; do not silently relabel it a trend swing to move the objective farther away.
- New tight congestion develops after a fill: no new entry/add risk inside it; manage the existing position by its thesis/stop and declared no-progress policy, not an automatic forced exit or automatic HOLD.
- Planned adverse add zone reached, thesis intact, but combined stop risk exceeds the committed cap: no ADD_CANDIDATE despite better average price. Existing position management remains separate.
- Position appears to hit a target, but an exit is already pending: report pending action/state; do not issue duplicate profit-taking or mark the trade closed without an event.
- Entry-plan expiry passes after an earlier confirmed fill: do not close merely because the entry opportunity expired; use the filled-position holding/exit policy.
- Historical bar touches both stop and target with no known sequence: flag ambiguity; do not count a winning exit.
