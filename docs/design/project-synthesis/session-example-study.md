# Session behavior: examples and counterexamples

Resumed source study, 15 October 2026 IST. Requirements synthesis only. Three completed book charts were visually inspected alongside their explanations. The cutoff questions below reconstruct what would need to be assessed; later bars and source outcomes were visible during study. This is development interpretation, **not an independent masked replay**, a numerical fill simulation or evidence of an NSE edge. Exact tradable prices are not estimated from raster charts.

## Opening balance that later changes character

Reversals Figure 19.1, printed pp.379–380 / PDF 407–408: five-minute E-mini chart. The opening alternates between upper and lower tests, then declines into a lower balance before a late rally returns to the earlier area.

| Illustrated cutoff | Available structural question | What must remain unknown at that cutoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upper test near label 3, after initial low near 2 | Is this an inward upper-edge rotation, or accepted escape? What actual signal and remaining room exist? | That this test will not become the final high; the later lower balance and late rally |
| Lower test near label 4 | Does the lower-edge test support inward rotation under an enabled route, or is selling gaining acceptance? | The eventual session low and whether the bounce will carry across the range |
| Renewed upper test near 5 | What changed since the earlier upper test? Which range bounds were already available? | That 5 will ultimately be the day's high |
| Strong selling toward 6 | Has the outer range failed with meaningful acceptance? Is an initial escape still prospective, or should a real corrective test be assessed? | The completed decline, exact later reversal point and full-day reversal label |
| Late opposing movement from the lower area | Has current bullish evidence changed the intraday swing, and is enough room/time left under policy? | That the session will close back in the upper balance |

The source explicitly notes that its alternative opening-range projections did not precisely locate reversals in this example. Therefore opening-range arithmetic is a conditional objective hypothesis, not an exact turning-point detector. The label 3/4 double tests cannot be assigned the final high/low in real time merely because similar structures sometimes form session extremes. An eventual reversal-day close cannot justify an early long held through the entire intervening decline.

**Geometry consequence:** publish the currently selected bounds and projection before a new plan. Later expanded bounds can inform a later plan, not rewrite an earlier target. An inward range trade and a newly accepted breakout have different objectives even within the same session. A missed breakout is not retroactively filled; a subsequent test needs its own completed evidence and identity.

## Small opening range followed by sustained continuation

Reversals Figure 19.2, printed pp.381–382 / PDF 409–410: five-minute FCX chart. Unlike Figure 19.1, the early alternating activity gives way to sustained bullish progress with small corrections. Read the contrast rather than treating opening-range size as a sufficient entry rule.

- Near label 2, the source discusses bullish bars whose small bodies and tails require further verification. Two same-direction bars alone are insufficient universal confirmation.
- Near label 4, a lower-edge rejection and subsequent bullish progress provide different evidence. The project cannot assume its next trigger already filled at the signal close. A pattern resembling double-bottom continuation needs the real contextual/sequence definition of the enabled route.
- Near label 5, acceptance beyond the earlier high adds evidence. It cannot be used to upgrade a previously recorded label-4 decision retroactively. It can support the current breakout interpretation if prospective geometry remains viable.
- Later higher prices and limited corrections support continuation as they actually arrive. A bigger late correction is not known earlier; its eventual appearance does not supply an intraday holding deadline or a universal stop distance.

**Important source-reading distinction:** the discussion of doubling a protective stop's size for reversal concerns the order quantity needed to close and reverse a position, not permission to double its stop-price distance. This project implements neither behavior. Source references to a large gap require verified previous-session price information in real use; a steep plotted moving average alone is not a reliable numerical gap definition.

**Geometry consequence:** a strong escape can require wider initial structural protection; the first qualified correction can offer different risk geometry. Do not apply an arbitrary one-bar stop to every breakout, or force every later pullback to protect the full origin of the morning move. No source stock ticks/percentages or occurrence rates are transplanted into NIFTY.

## H2 and gap strength that do not lock the day bullish

Reversals Figure 19.4, printed pp.384–385 / PDF 412–413: AAPL chart containing three sessions. The source describes an H2 breakout pullback at label 7, a subsequent higher high/rejection near 8, opposing momentum toward 9 and later bearish attempts near 10/11.

At 7, a gap and H2 do not prove an established whole-day bull trend or guarantee a swing projection. Assess the current initiative, upper opposition, actual correction and permissible objective. The completed failure near 8 must not be used to declare every otherwise eligible label-7 trade invalid in hindsight. A losing trade and a poorly supported trade are different claims.

At 8, assess new rejection evidence against the bullish continuation premise. The source later calls this a final-flag breakout; the skill must distinguish an anticipated late-flag failure from a failure actually observed now. An opposing entry still requires an enabled route, prospective trigger and complete geometry; closing a long does not automatically authorize a short.

At 9, a new low with strong opposing momentum need not be a bargain long. Assess whether selling still has continuation support and whether the move is entering two-sided balance. Later bearish resolution cannot be assumed inside that balance. Source explanations about trapped positions are inferred mechanisms, not observed inventory supplied by OHLC.

**Geometry consequence:** protect the actual failed initiative/test under the appropriate route; consider the first substantial opposing area before a farther leg projection. Label-7 stop placement and label-8 reversal geometry need not match. Preserve the original plan if filled, then manage cited premise defeat under its policy. Do not relabel the same pending plan, move its objective to rescue it, or invent a reversal fill.

## Midday and close: the source conflict to retain

Trends ch25, PDF 455–457, describes morning strength followed by prolonged balance and potential late resumption. It also describes successful opposite-direction late moves. The adaptation must therefore retain the morning initiative as background while judging the **actual** escape in either direction. It cannot turn 'morning trend often resumes' into a mandatory directional gate or invent the late trap before it happens.

[Brooks's breakout-holding discussion](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/ask-al/breakouts/) distinguishes scalp expectations from accepting corrections in a swing, and discusses reassessing late balance. This supports choosing trade intent before entry. It does not supply a universal US-to-NSE clock conversion, or permission for the advanced order styles described there.

The remaining NSE session policy must declare entry cutoff, holding cutoff/close handling and observation continuity. Near close, a good pattern may be excluded because the intended move lacks permitted time; report policy exclusion separately from bad context. Never shrink protection to force late participation or assume overnight permission. A cutoff is a configured session constraint, not a forecast of how many bars the move needs.

## Requirements changed by this study

Retain causal opening-range bounds; compare opening size with supplied past completed daily ranges without predictive percentages; review both continuation and opposing acceptance; preserve prior late-session structure when available; avoid range-middle chasing and tight congestion; select intent, structural protection and one supported objective before entry. Keep these within existing context/evidence/geometry records. No new indicator, strategy ID or runtime service is needed.
